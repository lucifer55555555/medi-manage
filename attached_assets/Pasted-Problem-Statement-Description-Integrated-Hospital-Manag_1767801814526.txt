Problem Statement Description
Integrated Hospital Management System for OPD Queuing, Bed Availability, Patient Admission, and Inventory Control

Public hospitals in metropolitan cities like Delhi face persistent operational challenges related to overcrowded OPDs, inefficient patient flow, limited visibility of bed availability, delays in patient admission, and suboptimal inventory management of medicines and consumables. These issues adversely impact service delivery, patient satisfaction, and optimal utilization of healthcare resources.

1. OPD Queuing and Patient Flow Management

Outpatient Departments (OPDs) in government hospitals experience high patient footfall, leading to long waiting times, overcrowding, and uneven load distribution across departments. Currently, OPD registrations and consultations are largely handled through manual or semi-digital processes, lacking real-time queue monitoring and predictive analytics.

There is a strong need for a technology-driven queuing model that can:

Dynamically manage patient queues based on department, doctor availability, and consultation time

Reduce waiting time through intelligent token allocation

Provide real-time visibility to patients and hospital administrators

Support appointment-based and walk-in patient handling

Applying queuing theory models (e.g., M/M/1, M/M/c) can significantly improve OPD throughput and service efficiency.

2. Bed Availability and Patient Admission Management

Hospitals often lack a real-time, centralized view of bed occupancy, resulting in delays during patient admission, inefficient bed utilization, and challenges in emergency case handling. Manual tracking leads to data inconsistencies and poor coordination between OPDs, wards, and emergency units.

A hospital-based digital solution is required to:

Track real-time bed availability across departments (ICU, general ward, specialty wards)

Automate admission, transfer, and discharge workflows

Enable priority-based bed allocation for emergency and critical cases

Provide dashboards for hospital administrators and clinicians

Such a system can reduce admission delays and improve overall hospital capacity management.

3. Medicine Dispensation and Consumables Management

Dispensation of medicines and consumables is a critical hospital function, yet it is often fragmented across pharmacies, stores, and departments. Challenges include:

Stock-outs and overstocking

Manual record-keeping

Lack of real-time consumption tracking

Expiry-related wastage

An integrated Inventory Management Module is required to:

Track procurement, storage, and dispensation of medicines and consumables

Monitor real-time stock levels and expiry dates

Generate automated reorder alerts based on consumption patterns

Support audit trails and regulatory compliance

4. Integration with NIC Modules and City-Wide Systems

The National Informatics Centre (NIC) has already developed multiple healthcare-related modules; however, their implementation in Delhi hospitals is yet to begin. These modules require customization, deployment, and seamless integration with hospital-level systems.

A hospital-based modular solution is ideal, which:

Operates independently at the hospital level

Integrates with existing NIC-developed systems

Scales to a city-wide healthcare management platform

Supports interoperability standards and secure data exchange

5. Proposed Technological Approach

The proposed solution involves developing an Integrated Hospital Management System (IHMS) with the following key components:

OPD Queuing and Appointment Management System

Bed Availability and Admission Management System

Pharmacy and Inventory Management System

Analytics and Decision Support Dashboards

Integration Layer for NIC and city-wide health platforms

The system will leverage:

Real-time data processing

Predictive analytics using queuing models

Role-based access control

Scalable and interoperable architecture

6. Expected Outcomes

Reduced OPD waiting time and improved patient experience

Optimized bed utilization and faster admissions

Efficient medicine and consumable inventory control

Better administrative decision-making through real-time dashboards

A scalable foundation for city-wide healthcare digitization
